# Creative Studio — the audio lane (sprint-4 scope + designer commission)

**Status:** scope agreed, designer commission ready to send · **Date:** 2026-08-07 · **Target:** sprint 4
**Decision:** the full lane, generated music out — voiceover from `narration`, an imported music bed, a film-wide mix. Chosen over VO-only (music would force a lane redraw — the Review stale-by-omission rework, again) and over bed-only (the field everyone authors would stay dead another sprint).
**Send the commission mid-sprint-3** — the designer's redraw latency gated R3 once already; sprint 4 must not be born blocked.

## 0. Measured ground truth (2026-08-07, `creative-suite-sprint2` local)

- **`narration` goes nowhere.** Validated (`creativeStudioService.ts:550`), projected, and authored on every surface — typed by users, generated by the storyboard planner (it is in the planner's output schema, `storyboardPrompt.ts:33`), revisable by the scene assist — with zero downstream consequence. It is `visualPrompt` before Slice P: a fully-authored field waiting to become real.
- **The stage is already unmuted** — provider audio in takes is audible today; the v1.1 §2 item landed.
- **The render pipeline is audio-ready.** Per-segment A/V pairs, `anullsrc` for silent segments, and R2's trim-aware audio filters (`renderService.ts:456` `audioFilterWithTrim`). New tracks are an `amix` insertion into a tested pipeline, not new plumbing.
- **Zero TTS or music machinery exists** — the only "speech" in the app is dictation input (`SpeechInputButton`). A voice role is genuinely new.

## 1. The three sources, and what each costs

- **Voiceover** — per-scene TTS from `narration`. The one **new media role**: `voice` joins `storyboard`/`image`/`video` in the routing model, with an adapter, capability detection, and a models-panel row — the expensive part of the whole lane. **One film-wide voice**, selected as a project-level setting like aspect ratio; per-scene character voices are out. Generation is a **paid batch action through the existing review-modal pattern** — scenes listed, honest-cost line, no new consent surface — and cached by `(narration, voice, route)` so an unchanged scene never re-bills.
- **Music bed** — **one imported file per project**: file picker → project-level asset (the Slice-P pool pattern, an `audio` collection). No provider, no licensing exposure — the user brings the track and the rights. Fade-out at film end.
- **Clip audio** — already exists and is audible; it gains **per-clip on/off** and participates in the mix. This makes the cut editor honest about audio it already renders.

VO audio persists per scene in its own collection (`voiceover`), never as a take — it must never touch `selectedAssetId`, `assetIds`, or review state (the Slice-P discipline, applied to a second media kind).

## 2. The mix, deliberately primitive

Three film-wide gains — clip audio, voiceover, bed — plus **one auto-duck**: bed and clip audio dip under active VO (ffmpeg sidechain-style; the exact filter and its LGPL status are spike items, not assumptions). No keyframes, no per-clip envelopes, no waveform editing.

Same shape as the colour model on purpose: few scalars, one fixed composition rule, conformance-testable — a golden-sample net for the mix belongs in the implementation plan the way the golden-pixel table served colour.

## 3. Timing keeps its religion

Timing stays **advisory, never enforced** — the settled Studio principle. A VO segment is placed at its clip's start. When narration runs longer than its clip, the mismatch is **surfaced** the way `PacingBar` surfaces duration drift — never silently truncated, never blocking. Whether overrun spills into the next clip, requests a re-time, or offers both is the designer's question (§6.2).

## 4. What the assistant does and doesn't do

Nothing new. Narration is already assistant-authored at both tiers — script proposals write it, the scene assist revises it — so the _writing_ half of voiceover ships with sprint 3. Voice selection and every generation trigger stay with the user; no audio tool enters the Brief allow-list; the assistant may never cause a paid TTS call. Same rule, same mechanism, no exceptions.

## 5. S-AUDIO-1 — the spike that gates planning

Half a day, same pattern as the video-capability spike; its outcome cannot change the lane's shape, only which adapter is written first.

1. Which TTS routes are reachable **with existing user keys** (OpenRouter audio endpoints? BytePlus/media-gateway?), their request/response shapes, formats, and length limits.
2. Whether the chosen duck filter (`sidechaincompress` or equivalent) survives the **LGPL-only** constraint the render pipeline already lives under.
3. Returned-duration behaviour — does the provider report VO duration, or do we measure on ingest (the `durationSeconds` validators' integer trap is already known from the cut model; VO durations are fractional by nature).

## 6. The designer's questions (the commission)

1. **Where does audio live in Review?** A track lane under the R3 timeline, a fifth inspector section (after takes/trim/frame/colour), or both — and what a scene looks like on the strip in each state: _with VO_, _without VO_, _VO stale_, _VO overrun_.
2. **Overrun behaviour** — spill into the next clip, suggest a re-time, or both as a user choice. §3 constrains the answer: advisory, never truncating.
3. **The mix surface** — three gains and one duck as a compact panel; where it sits, and the zero-state before any audio exists.
4. **The VO generate moment** — batch from Write (beside Draft storyboard?) or from Review; and how a **stale VO** (narration edited after generation) announces itself. Prior art exists and should be reused in register: the divergence chip's `Yours · edited by hand` wording.

## 7. Non-goals

Generated music (licensing and provider maturity — revisit with its own gate); sound effects; voice cloning (governance before capability); per-scene voices; per-clip audio keyframes or envelopes; waveform editing; multi-track; any change to how takes generate their own audio; any assistant-triggered audio generation.

## 8. Sequencing

Designer commission out **mid-sprint-3**; S-AUDIO-1 runs any time before sprint-4 planning; engineering lands the lane as **VO → bed → mix**, each slice independently revertible, with the VO slice's implementation plan written only after the spike and the returned drawings are in hand. Pairs with, but stays separate from, the sprint-4 export/destinations story.
