# New joiner runbook — Platform team

This runbook is the checklist a hiring manager works through for every new joiner on the Platform team. People Operations owns the document; the hiring manager owns the outcome. Nothing here replaces the company handbook, it only says who does what and when.

## 1. Before day one

### 1.1 Accounts and access

People Operations raises the account request five working days before the start date. The request creates the directory account, the mail account, and membership of the team distribution list. Nothing else is granted automatically.

Repository access is requested separately by the hiring manager, naming the exact repositories and the level needed. Blanket organisation-wide access is not granted to new joiners, including transfers from other teams.

Production access is never part of an onboarding request. It follows the standard access-review process once the joiner has completed the first month.

### 1.2 Hardware

The Infrastructure team images the laptop and ships it to the office the joiner is assigned to. Standard build is a 14-inch machine with 32 GB of memory; a larger build needs written justification from the hiring manager and is approved case by case.

Full-disk encryption is switched on during imaging and verified before handover. A laptop that fails the encryption check is not handed over, it goes back to Infrastructure.

Peripherals are drawn from office stock. Anything not in stock is ordered against the team's office-services cost centre, not against the joiner's own budget.

### 1.3 Desk and building access

Facilities assigns a desk in the team's zone and issues the building card on the morning of day one. Card collection is in person at reception; cards are not posted or handed to colleagues.

## 2. Day one

### 2.1 Morning

The hiring manager meets the joiner at reception at 09:30 and walks them through the floor, the meeting rooms, and the fire exits. This is not delegated to the buddy.

Reception hands over the building card and the laptop. The joiner signs the equipment receipt on the spot.

### 2.2 Afternoon

The joiner sets up the laptop with Infrastructure available on the support channel. Nothing is installed by Infrastructure remotely; the joiner drives it so they know how it works.

## 3. First week

### 3.1 Mandatory training

Every new joiner must finish the information-security course before the laptop is issued for use with company data. The security team runs the course twice a month and publishes dates a quarter ahead. A joiner starting between course dates works on public material only until the next session.

The data-handling module is separate and is due within the first three weeks. It is shorter, self-paced, and repeated annually for everyone.

### 3.2 Buddy programme

Every new joiner is assigned a buddy from their own squad, named before day one and introduced on day one. The buddy answers the small questions that are awkward to raise with a manager, and is the joiner's first stop for anything about how the team actually works day to day. People Operations coordinates the pairing and keeps the roster.

The commitment runs for six weeks, with a mandatory 30-minute check-in every Friday. Either side can ask People Operations for a different pairing, once, without giving a reason.

## 4. First month

### 4.1 Ramp-up expectations

A first change merged in week one is a healthy sign, but nothing is measured on it. The real checkpoint is the end of month one, where the hiring manager and the joiner write down what the joiner is now expected to own.

### 4.2 Feedback

The hiring manager holds a written check-in at the end of week two and again at the end of month one. Both are shared with People Operations. Anything raised as a concern is written down at the time, not saved for the probation review.

### 4.3 Probation review

Probation runs for two months from the start date. The review is a written document from the hiring manager, countersigned by the joiner, and it says one of three things: confirm, extend once by one month, or end.

There is no fourth option and no informal extension. A review that has not happened by the end of the probation window counts as a confirmation, so the date sits in the hiring manager's calendar from day one.

An extension states in writing what specifically has to be different by the end of it. "More of the same, faster" is not an extension criterion and People Operations will send it back.

## 5. Tooling

### 5.1 Local environment

The joiner works through the repository's own setup guide rather than a separate onboarding copy, because a separate copy goes stale and nobody notices. If the setup guide is wrong, fixing it is the joiner's first change and the buddy reviews it.

Setup is expected to take most of a day. Two days is normal on a new machine. Longer than that is a signal about the setup guide, not about the joiner, and the hiring manager raises it.

### 5.2 Access to shared tooling

The issue tracker, the design tool, and the incident tool are all requested through the IT service catalogue. Each has a different owner and a different turnaround, so all three are raised on day one rather than when first needed.

The observability stack is read-only for the first month. Write access follows the same access-review process as production, because the two share an approval path.

### 5.3 What not to install

Nothing that stores company data on a third-party service the company has not signed with. If a tool would help, it goes through the paid-software route in the service catalogue, however small the subscription.

Personal browser extensions with access to internal pages are the most common quiet breach of this rule. The security course covers it; the buddy is expected to repeat it anyway.

## 6. Team rituals

### 6.1 Standing meetings

The team meets weekly on Monday, moving to 09:30 from 1 July. The joiner attends from week one and is not expected to have anything to report for the first two weeks.

Design review runs fortnightly and is optional for the first month. Incident review is attended by whoever was involved, plus anyone who wants to learn, and the joiner is welcome from day one.

### 6.2 On-call

New joiners do not go on call during probation. After confirmation, the first rotation is shadowed: the joiner carries the pager alongside an experienced engineer for one full week before taking a rotation alone.

Shadowing is not optional and does not compress. A joiner who has not shadowed does not take a rotation, regardless of previous experience elsewhere.

### 6.3 Written culture

Decisions are written down where the people affected will find them, not announced in a meeting and left there. The joiner is asked to write up their own onboarding experience in week three, which is where most improvements to this runbook come from.

## 7. Common mistakes

The most common mistake a hiring manager makes is raising the access requests late. Five working days before the start date is the deadline, not the target, and the account request is the one that blocks everything else.

The second is treating the buddy as a substitute for management. The buddy handles the small questions; expectations, feedback, and the probation review stay with the hiring manager.

The third is a silent first month. If the joiner has had no written feedback by the end of week two, the process has already gone wrong, and it is recoverable then and much harder later.

## 8. Ownership

People Operations owns this runbook and reviews it twice a year. Change requests go to People Operations with the specific paragraph quoted, not a general note that a section is unclear.
